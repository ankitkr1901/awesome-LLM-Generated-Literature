
# Knowledge Cutoffs, Temporal Knowledge Boundaries, and Citation Reliability in Large Language Models

## Abstract

Large language models are increasingly used to support academic literature reviews, but generated references may not respect a researcher's intended publication-date boundary. This study investigates whether adding an explicit temporal instruction changes the publication-date compliance of generated literature reviews.

A controlled comparative design was used, consisting of five matched prompt pairs and ten generated reviews. Condition A used a standard literature-review prompt, while Condition B included an explicit instruction to cite only scholarly works published on or before 31 December 2024. A total of 99 citation instances were extracted and assessed.

Condition A contained 15 post-cutoff citations among 51 references, producing a post-cutoff rate of 29.41%. Condition B contained no post-cutoff citations among 48 references. The mean paired reduction was 29.64 percentage points. A sensitivity analysis restricted to bibliographically matched references retained a mean paired reduction of 27.14 percentage points.

The findings suggest that explicit publication-date instructions may improve observable temporal compliance in generated literature reviews. However, the small sample and unresolved bibliographic cases require cautious interpretation. The study measures compliance with an explicit date constraint rather than the model's internal knowledge cutoff.

**Keywords:** Large language models, knowledge cutoff, temporal knowledge, citation reliability, literature review, hallucination, bibliographic verification.

# 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in academic research for summarizing literature, identifying relevant studies, and drafting literature reviews. These capabilities can reduce the time required for preliminary research. However, the reliability of generated academic references remains an important concern.

One particular challenge involves temporal knowledge boundaries. A researcher may require a literature review to include only studies published before a specified date. A generated review may nevertheless include references published after that date, potentially because the model does not consistently follow the intended temporal scope.

Citation reliability also involves more than publication dates. References may contain incorrect titles, author information, publication venues, or digital object identifiers (DOIs). A citation can refer to a real work while still containing inaccurate metadata.

This study examines whether an explicit publication-date boundary instruction can improve the temporal compliance of generated literature reviews. It also examines bibliographic verification outcomes to distinguish date compliance from reference accuracy.

## 1.1 Research Problem

When a language model generates a literature review without a clearly stated publication-date restriction, it may include works published after the researcher's intended cutoff date. This can create problems for historical reviews, systematic literature searches, and research requiring a fixed temporal scope.

The study therefore investigates whether an explicit date-boundary instruction reduces the occurrence of post-cutoff citations.

## 1.2 Research Question

Does adding an explicit publication-date boundary instruction reduce post-cutoff citations in literature reviews generated by a large language model, and how does this affect bibliographic reliability?

## 1.3 Research Objectives

1. Compare citation-date compliance under standard and explicitly constrained prompts.
2. Identify references published after the specified cutoff date.
3. Assess the bibliographic consistency of generated references.
4. Compare outcomes across five matched prompt pairs.
5. Examine whether the observed pattern remains when unresolved references are excluded.

## 1.4 Scope

The study uses a publication-date cutoff of 31 December 2024, inclusive. It compares two prompting conditions using ten generated literature reviews. The findings concern observable compliance with the stated temporal boundary and do not directly establish the model's internal training-data cutoff.
